Shoe Size Converter

Shoe sizes are not a measurement of your foot. They are a count of units along the last, the mould a shoe is built around, and every region counts differently. The US and UK use the barleycorn, one third of an inch per full size. Europe uses the Paris point, two thirds of a centimetre. Japan simply prints your foot length in centimetres. This converter takes a size in any of those systems and returns the equivalent in all the others, plus the foot length the size was designed for.

It covers men's, women's and kids' ranges separately, because a US men's 9 and a US women's 9 are not the same shoe. Everything is calculated from a fixed reference table, so the same input always produces the same answer, and no data is stored.

How the Sizing Systems Differ

The US and UK scales share a unit but not a starting point. A barleycorn is one third of an inch, about 0.847 cm, and each full size adds one. The UK counts from zero and the US from one, which is why the two numbers never match: a US men's 10 is a UK 9.5, a gap of half a size. Women's scales diverge further, because the US women's scale starts about 1.5 sizes above the men's scale on the same physical length.

Europe works differently. A Paris point is two thirds of a centimetre, roughly 0.667 cm, and EU sizes count Paris points along the last. The scale is unisex: an EU 42 is an EU 42 whether the shoe is cut for a man or a woman. Consecutive EU sizes step by 0.5, which gives you about 0.33 cm of extra length per half size, a finer grid than the barleycorn's 0.847 cm per full size.

That difference in resolution is why conversions never line up exactly. Half a US size is 0.42 cm, while half an EU size is 0.33 cm, so one system's half step falls between the other system's steps. Any converter that promises exact matches is rounding. This one snaps to the nearest half size and tells you when your input sat between two.

Japan, and most of Asia, skips the counting entirely and prints foot length in centimetres, sometimes called Mondopoint when it is written as 260 rather than 26. If you know that number, you can skip every conversion chart and match it directly, which is why measuring your foot is more reliable than converting a size you were told once.

Worked Conversions

A US men's 10 sits on a foot length of about 26.7 cm. Read against the men's table that is a UK 9.5 and an EU 43.5. The same 26.7 cm against the women's table is a US women's 10.5, a UK 8.5 and an EU 42. Two different numbers, one foot: the length is identical, only the scale changes.

An EU 38 is a small size, so it is read against the women's table, giving a US women's 7, a UK 5 and 23.7 cm. Ask for a men's equivalent and the honest answer is that the men's range barely goes that low, which is why the converter shows a dash instead of inventing a number.

A measured foot of 26 cm lands exactly on the US men's 9 line, which is a UK 8.5 and an EU 42.5. Add the half centimetre of clearance a closed shoe needs and the same foot reads as 26.5 cm, halfway between the 9 and the 9.5, which is where the converter snaps up and tells you it did.

Measuring Your Foot at Home

  • Measure in the evening. Feet swell through the day and can be half a size larger by then, which is the state they will be in when you wear the shoes.
  • Stand up and put weight on the foot. A seated measurement is shorter and will understate the size you need.
  • Put a sheet of paper against a wall, heel touching the wall, and mark the tip of the longest toe. Measure both feet and use the larger one.
  • Wear the socks you plan to wear with the shoe. Thick wool socks can add 0.5 cm.
  • Add clearance: about 0.5 cm for closed shoes, up to 1 cm for running shoes, nothing extra for sandals that are meant to be close-fitting.

Why the Same Number Fits Differently

A size is a length, not a shape. Two shoes labelled US men's 10 can differ in width, arch position, heel volume and toe box, because each brand builds on its own last. A running shoe with a wide toe box at 10 can feel roomier than a dress shoe at 10.5 from a brand that runs narrow.

Material matters too. Full-grain leather stretches about half a size over the first months of wear; mesh and synthetic uppers barely move. That is the argument for buying leather snug and mesh with clearance, not the other way round.

Rounding adds the last wrinkle. Brands map their internal lasts onto national scales by hand, and some round up, some down. This is why a single size can fit true in one brand and half a size small in another, and why the conversion chart is a starting point rather than a guarantee. If you are ordering from a brand you have never worn, converting your measured foot length is more reliable than converting the size printed inside your old shoes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a US women's size 8 the same as a men's size 8?

No. A women's 8 fits roughly the same foot as a men's 6.5, a gap of about 1.5 sizes. The unit is the same barleycorn, but the two scales start at different points. If you are buying a unisex shoe, subtract 1.5 from your women's size to get the men's size to order.

Why does an EU 42 fit like a US 9 in one brand and a 9.5 in another?

Because EU sizes are nominal and brands round them differently onto their own lasts. Two brands can both print 42 while one internal length is a few millimetres longer. Foot length in centimetres is the only figure that survives the trip between brands.

Should I size up for running shoes?

Usually half a size above your everyday shoe. Feet swell during exercise and slide forward on descents, so you want a thumb's width between the longest toe and the end of the shoe. Walking shoes and leather dress shoes do not need that allowance and often feel loose if you add it.

How do kids' shoe sizes convert as they grow?

Kids climb about one US size every three to four months as toddlers, then roughly one size every six months until around age five. The scales run 1 to 13 and then restart at 1 for youth sizes, so a child outgrowing a 13 moves to a youth 1. Converting to EU avoids the restart confusion entirely, because that scale keeps counting.

What is a Paris point?

The unit behind EU sizing: two thirds of a centimetre, about 0.26 inches. EU numbers count Paris points along the last and step in halves, which gives a finer grid than the barleycorn. Because it measures the last rather than the foot, your EU size is usually one number above the length of your foot in centimetres.

My foot measures 26 cm, what size should I order?

That is a US men's 9, a UK 8.5, an EU 42.5, or a US women's 10. Add the 0.5 cm of clearance a closed shoe needs and you are at 26.5 cm, mid-way between the 9 and 9.5, so order the 9.5. For sandals, where the foot can spread, the 9 is the better call.
