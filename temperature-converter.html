The three common temperature scales use different reference points, and the conversion between them trips people up mainly on negative numbers and on the Fahrenheit offset.

Celsius to Fahrenheit is F = C × 9/5 + 32, and the reverse is C = (F − 32) × 5/9. The scales coincide at −40, which is the one value worth remembering as a sanity check. 100°C boils water, 212°F is the same point, and 37°C body temperature is 98.6°F. Kelvin adds 273.15 to Celsius with no degree symbol, since it is an absolute scale starting at zero.

The mental shortcut people use — double it and add 30 — is accurate near room temperature and drifts badly at the extremes. For weather, a useful anchor set is 0°C = 32°F, 10°C = 50°F, 20°C = 68°F and 30°C = 86°F, each exactly correct to the nearest degree.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

Multiply by 9, divide by 5, then add 32. 25°C becomes 45 + 32 = 77°F.

At what temperature are Celsius and Fahrenheit equal?

−40 degrees. Below that point, Fahrenheit readings are higher than Celsius; above it, the reverse.

Is Kelvin measured in degrees?

No. The unit is simply kelvin, written without a degree symbol, because it is an absolute scale.

Why is 98.6°F used for body temperature?

It derives from an 1851 estimate of 37°C. Modern measurements put average body temperature slightly lower, around 36.6°C.
