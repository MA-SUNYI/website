The underlying step is straightforward long division. Take 5/16: 16 into 50 goes three times (48), remainder 2; 16 into 20 goes once (16), remainder 4; 16 into 40 goes twice (32), remainder 8; 16 into 80 goes five times (80), remainder 0. The answer is 0.3125, terminating after four places because 16 = 2⁴.

An improper fraction such as 9/4 converts to 2.25, which is the same as saying 2¼. Some contexts require the decimal, some require the mixed number, and a few require both — test scores and tool sizes are commonly given as the decimal, while recipes and carpentry often stay in fractions. Frequently Asked Questions

What is 1/3 as a decimal?

0.3333…, repeating without end. It cannot be written exactly as a finite decimal, so any quoted figure such as 0.33 or 0.3333 is an approximation.

How do I convert a fraction to a decimal without a calculator?

Use long division, or scale the denominator to a power of ten. If the denominator divides into 10, 100 or 1000, multiply both parts to reach that denominator: 3/4 becomes 75/100, which reads directly as 0.75.

What does a decimal that never ends mean?

It means the fraction's denominator has a prime factor other than 2 or 5. The decimal still represents an exact value; only its written form is incomplete.

Is it better to keep a fraction or convert to a decimal?

Keep fractions when exactness matters, such as in algebra or recipe scaling. Convert to decimals when measuring, comparing magnitudes, or entering values into software.
