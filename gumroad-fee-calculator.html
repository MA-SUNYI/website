How Gumroad Splits a Purchase

Four deductions can land on one checkout, and only the first is advertised.

Commission — ten percent of the total plus a flat fifty cents on any direct purchase: a link in your newsletter, a button on your own site, your profile. Past $20,000 of paid volume inside a single calendar month, fresh direct purchases that month fall to five percent plus the same fifty cents. Volume is measured monthly, so a creator who clears $20,000 across a year never sees the lower tier.

Payment handling — separate from the commission. Domestic cards land near 2.9 percent plus $0.30. A PayPal checkout is billed at PayPal's own schedule, roughly 3.49 percent plus $0.49, and cross-border cards or currency conversion add about 1.5 percent and 1 percent more.

Marketplace rate — a flat 30 percent whenever a stranger discovers the product inside Gumroad's own storefront. Handling is already inside that figure, so 30 percent is genuinely the entire deduction.

Withdrawal charge — bank transfers are free above the payout minimum, PayPal takes 2 percent and US instant transfers take 3 percent. It comes off the money being moved, not off the sticker.

Written as a formula: net = (sticker − commission − handling) × (1 − withdrawal rate). Subtract your cost per unit and you have profit; the break-even figure is the lowest sticker that makes that profit zero.

Worked example: a $29 course, direct card purchase, bank withdrawal, $3 of cost. Commission is $2.90 + $0.50 = $3.40 and handling is $0.84 + $0.30 = $1.14, so $4.54 leaves the transaction, 15.7 percent of the sticker. Net is $24.46 and profit is $21.46. Route the identical $29 through Discover and Gumroad keeps $8.70, leaving $20.30, or $17.30 after cost — a $4.16 swing on one unit.

Why Cheap Downloads Give Up So Much

Eighty cents of fixed charges do not shrink with the sticker, so the cheapest items carry the heaviest rate. A $4 template that costs nothing to deliver still surrenders about 33 percent. Creators selling a $7 planner rarely notice either: the price looks healthy, the net is $5.30, and once a $3 ad click is counted the unit contributes $2.30.

Three ways out, in order of effect. Bundle related files into a $29 pack so the fixed charges spread across a bigger number. Raise the sticker and add a bonus rather than discounting into the fixed-fee zone. Or move the cheapest item to a free download that collects an email address, then sell the paid product to that list — a direct purchase from a warm audience pays 10 percent, not 30.

The Marketplace Rate Is a Customer Acquisition Cost

Thirty percent reads as punitive until it is compared with what a stranger is worth. On a $50 digital product, Discover keeps $15; a paid ad click that converts at 3 percent costs roughly $10 to $30 in spend for the same customer, with no guarantee of conversion. Below about $4.68 the marketplace rate is actually cheaper than a direct purchase, since it carries no fixed charge at all.

The mistake is absorbing the difference silently. If a meaningful share of volume arrives through Discover, either lift the sticker for that channel or accept a lower contribution per unit when planning the launch — do not average the two rates and assume the blend holds.

Limits of This Estimate

Gumroad acts as merchant of record, so sales tax and VAT are collected from the buyer on top and remitted for you; they never touch your net and are not modelled here. Affiliate commissions are split proportionally and quietly raise the blend, chargebacks carry their own charge, and any ad spend is your own line item. Rates move without ceremony — verify the current schedule on Gumroad's own help pages before you commit a price list, and fold average acquisition cost into the cost field if you want a number that reflects a real launch.

Questions Creators Ask

How much does Gumroad take from a $25 sale?

Commission is $2.50 + $0.50 = $3.00 and card handling is $0.725 + $0.30 = $1.03. That is $4.03, or 16.1 percent, leaving $20.97 rather than the $22.50 the headline rate implies.

Is the 10 percent commission all inclusive?

No. Handling is billed on top at about 2.9 percent plus $0.30 on cards, or PayPal's schedule. Only the 30 percent Discover rate bundles handling in.

What is the Discover fee?

A flat 30 percent when a new customer finds you in Gumroad's marketplace. On a $50 file that is $15.00 against roughly $7.25 on a direct card purchase.

Can a cheap product actually lose money?

It can once delivery or support costs are real. Eighty cents of fixed charges on a $5 download leaves $3.55, so a printed insert or a refund eats the margin immediately. Under about $4.68, Discover's flat rate is the cheaper channel.

When does the 5 percent tier start?

After $20,000 of paid volume in one calendar month, applied only to new direct purchases in that month. Discover is unaffected and the fifty cents stays.

Are withdrawals charged?

Bank transfers are free above the minimum balance. PayPal costs 2 percent, US instant transfers 3 percent, and this tool applies whichever you select.
