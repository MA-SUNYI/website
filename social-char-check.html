Each social platform enforces a different limit, and the differences matter when one post is scheduled across several networks. The constraint is almost always the shortest limit in the set.

Current approximate limits: X/Twitter 280 characters, Instagram caption 2,200, Facebook post 63,206, LinkedIn post 3,000, TikTok caption 2,200, YouTube title 100 and description 5,000. A post written for Instagram will be truncated on X and cut to the first line on many feeds, where only the opening 100 to 125 characters are shown before the more link.

That preview limit is the one most writers underestimate. Regardless of the maximum, the first 125 characters determine whether anyone expands the post, which makes them the most valuable characters in the whole update. Writing the hook first and the rest afterwards inverts the usual drafting order and usually improves results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the character limits for major platforms?

X/Twitter 280, Instagram caption 2,200, LinkedIn 3,000, Facebook 63,206, TikTok 2,200. Limits change, so confirm against the platform before scheduling.

Why is my long caption cut off?

Feeds show only the opening portion — often about 125 characters — before the more link. The rest exists but is not visible without expansion.

Do hashtags count toward the limit?

Yes. Hashtags, mentions, emoji and URLs all consume characters, and some platforms count a URL at a fixed length.

Should I use fewer hashtags on Instagram?

Current guidance suggests three to five relevant hashtags outperform large blocks. Hashtag count does not affect reach as much as relevance does.
