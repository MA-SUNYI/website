The area of a circle is π multiplied by the radius squared. With a diameter instead, halve it first — the most frequent error in this calculation, and the one that produces a result four times too large rather than a small rounding difference.

For a circle with radius 7 cm, the area is π × 49 ≈ 153.94 cm². For a circle with diameter 14 cm, the radius is 7 cm and the answer is identical; using 14 as the radius instead would give 615.75 cm², exactly four times the correct figure. Whenever a result looks implausibly big, check whether a diameter was substituted for a radius.

Pi is irrational, so every area is an approximation. Three decimal places in the result is realistic for practical work; for engineering drawings, keep the calculation in terms of π until the final step so intermediate rounding does not compound. The circumference formula, 2πr, is often confused with the area formula — circumference is a length, area is squared, and the units alone tell you which you have.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find the area if I only know the diameter?

Divide the diameter by two to get the radius, then square it and multiply by π. A 10 cm diameter means a 5 cm radius and an area of about 78.54 cm².

What is the difference between area and circumference?

Area measures the space inside the circle in square units; circumference measures the distance around it in linear units. Area is πr², circumference is 2πr.

How accurate should I be with π?

Using 3.14 introduces about 0.05% error, which is fine for most practical uses. Use the calculator's full precision for anything structural or financial.

Why is the area expressed in square units?

Because it is a two-dimensional measure: two lengths multiplied together. Reporting cm instead of cm² is a common marking error in school work.
