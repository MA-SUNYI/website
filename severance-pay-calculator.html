Severance Pay Calculator

Severance offers arrive with a deadline and a formula buried in a policy document, and the gross number on the letter is not the amount that reaches your account. This calculator applies the standard weeks-per-year formula, adds the PTO payout and any extra lump sum, then subtracts the withholding that actually comes out. It is an estimate of the package, not a legal opinion — the offer letter and your state rules decide the rest.

Result:

Weekly pay is annual salary divided by 52. PTO is valued at salary divided by 260 working days. Pick "Medicare only" if your salary already passed the Social Security wage base of $184,500 for the year.

How Severance Is Calculated

Almost every US severance policy is built from the same three inputs: weekly pay, a number of weeks granted per year of service, and a cap on the total. The formula is short enough to do by hand:

Weekly pay = annual base salary ÷ 52
Weeks granted = weeks per year × years of service, capped
Severance = weekly pay × weeks granted

Worked example: a $96,000 salary is $1,846.15 a week. Seven years at two weeks per year gives 14 weeks, which is below the usual 26-week ceiling, so severance is 14 × $1,846.15 = $25,846.15. Add 12 unused PTO days at $369.23 a day and the gross package is $30,276.92. With 22% federal withholding and 7.65% FICA, roughly $8,977 comes out and about $21,300 lands in the account. That is the number to plan around, and it is 30% smaller than the headline.

The cap does most of the work for long-tenured employees, and it is the first thing to look for in a policy. Two weeks per year at 20 years is 40 weeks on paper, but a 26-week cap turns that into six months of pay. A $150,000 salary at 15 years and three weeks per year would produce 45 weeks uncapped; the same 26-week cap reduces it to $75,000 instead of $129,808.

One week per year is the typical floor for individual contributors. Two weeks per year is common for managers and longer-tenured staff, and three to four weeks per year shows up in executive agreements. A flat "two months regardless of tenure" policy also exists and is worse for anyone past five years.

Salary does the rest. At one week per year, a $60,000 employee with three years receives $3,461.54 gross; a $120,000 employee with eleven years at two weeks per year receives $50,769.23 before the cap and before PTO. Severance scales with both inputs, which is why the same layoff announcement produces wildly different cheques on the same team.

What Else Usually Sits in the Package

The weeks-of-pay number is rarely the whole offer, and comparing two offers on that figure alone is how people leave money behind.

Unused PTO. Often paid at your regular rate whether or not the policy requires it. In states like California, accrued vacation is treated as wages and must be paid at separation; elsewhere it depends on the handbook. Twelve days at $96,000 is worth $4,430.

COBRA subsidy. An employer-paid health premium period is worth the monthly premium times the number of months, and it can easily exceed a month of severance. Price it against the marketplace plan you would otherwise buy.

Prorated bonus. If the layoff lands after a strong year, a prorated bonus is a normal ask and sometimes already in the policy. Get the calculation in writing rather than a verbal assurance.

Equity and outplacement. Accelerated vesting has a real cash value that depends on the share price, and outplacement services are worth what you would pay for them, not what the vendor charges the company.

The Tax Layer, and Why 22% Is Not Your Rate

Severance is taxable wages. Employers generally withhold federal income tax on it as supplemental wages at a flat 22% for amounts under $1 million, plus Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. State withholding applies where the state has an income tax, and several states use their own supplemental rate.

Two consequences worth understanding before the cheque clears. First, 22% is a prepayment, not your bracket. If your marginal rate is 24% or 32%, the difference is settled when you file, and a large lump sum received in a year with little other income can produce a refund instead. Second, the Social Security portion stops once wages pass the annual wage base, which is $184,500 for 2026 — if your salary already crossed it, severance carries only the 1.45% Medicare piece. That is why the calculator offers both FICA settings.

Some employers will pay severance as salary continuation instead of a lump sum. The gross is the same, but the withholding is computed as regular wages, which usually withholds more accurately, and the payment period sometimes keeps benefits active longer. Ask which method the offer uses before comparing net figures.

Where People Get This Wrong

Signing the release on day one. Most packages are conditional on releasing claims against the employer, and that release is what you are actually being paid for. If you are 40 or older, the ADEA gives you at least 21 days to consider it in an individual termination and 45 days in a group layoff, plus 7 days to revoke after signing. A deadline shorter than that is a negotiation signal, not a formality.

Treating 60 days of WARN notice as severance. The WARN Act requires 60 days' notice, or pay in lieu, for qualifying mass layoffs at larger employers. That obligation is separate from a discretionary severance policy; some employers try to fold the two together and call the result generous.

Budgeting the gross. A $30,000 package is roughly $21,000 after withholding and less than four months of a $5,500 monthly budget. Divide the net figure by actual monthly expenses before deciding you have time to be selective.

Assuming unemployment is unaffected. States differ on whether a lump sum delays benefits, and salary continuation is more likely to postpone them than a single payment. File anyway; the determination is made from facts, not from what the HR letter implies.

Finally, this tool cannot tell you what you are owed. Severance in the US is contractual or discretionary, and the policy document, any collective agreement, and the specific release language govern the outcome. Use the estimate to spot a package that is clearly below the stated formula, and take anything unusual to an employment attorney — most will review a release for a flat fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my employer legally required to pay severance?

Not under federal law. Private employers owe it only when a contract, union agreement, written policy or state rule creates the obligation, or when you negotiate it. WARN is a notice requirement for large layoffs, not a severance statute.

Is the 22% withholding on severance my actual tax rate?

No. It is a prepayment: employers commonly withhold a flat 22% federal plus 7.65% FICA on supplemental wages under $1 million. Your real liability is settled at filing at your marginal rate, so expect either a balance due or a refund depending on your other income that year.

What does signing the separation agreement cost me?

Usually your right to bring claims over the termination. If you are 40 or older, the ADEA gives you at least 21 days to review an individual release, 45 days for a group layoff, and 7 days to revoke after signing.

Does severance reduce unemployment benefits?

It depends on the state and on whether the money is a lump sum or salary continuation. Some states delay benefits while severance runs, others disregard a lump sum entirely. File and let the state determine it rather than assuming.

How is unused vacation time paid out at termination?

States like California require accrued vacation to be paid as wages at separation; elsewhere the handbook decides. This calculator uses salary divided by 260 working days, so swap in your company's daily rate if it differs.

Can a severance package include health coverage or equity?

Yes, and both change the value materially. A paid COBRA period is worth the premium times the months covered, and accelerated vesting has a cash value tied to the share price. Neither is in the formula-based figure, so price them separately.
