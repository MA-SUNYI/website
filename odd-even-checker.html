A number is even if it divides by two with no remainder, and odd otherwise. The test is simply whether the last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 — nothing about the size of the number changes that, which is why the check works instantly on values far too large for mental arithmetic.

Beyond the parities themselves, a handful of rules follow directly and are worth knowing. Even + even = even, odd + odd = even, even + odd = odd. Even × anything = even, while odd × odd = odd. These properties underpin a lot of programming: hashing, checksums and hash-table bucketing all lean on parity arithmetic at some level.

Negative numbers follow the same rule, since the sign does not affect divisibility. Zero is even under the standard definition, because 0 ÷ 2 = 0 with no remainder. The one edge case worth flagging is decimals — 2.5 is neither odd nor even, as parity is defined only for integers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is zero even or odd?

Zero is even. It divides by two with no remainder, and it behaves as an even number in every sum and product rule.

Are negative numbers odd or even?

The same rules apply. −4 is even and −7 is odd; the sign has no bearing on divisibility by two.

What is the fastest way to check a large number?

Look at the final digit only. If it is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 the number is even, and no other digit matters.

Do decimal numbers count as odd or even?

No. Parity is defined only for whole numbers, so a value like 3.0 counts as odd only after you confirm it is an integer.
