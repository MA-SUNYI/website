Text length converts between the measures people actually use: characters, characters without spaces, words, sentences and estimated reading time. Each answers a different question, and choosing the wrong one produces a confidently wrong answer.

Reading time is the most abused of these. The common assumption of 200 to 250 words per minute describes silent reading of straightforward prose. Technical material, legal text and non-native readers can run at half that speed. A 1,000-word article is roughly four to five minutes at a general reading pace, and eight to ten for dense documentation.

Characters without spaces is the figure print designers and form validators often want, while characters with spaces is what most web fields enforce. Including both in the output removes the guesswork, and it is the reason a dedicated tool beats a rough mental estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words per minute does an average reader read?

About 200 to 250 words per minute for general prose. Technical or dense material is often half that.

What is the difference between characters with and without spaces?

Spaces are characters, so the total with spaces is higher. Form fields usually count spaces; some print specifications do not.

How do I convert words to pages?

Roughly 500 words per page double-spaced and 250 for single-spaced academic formatting, though font and margins change the figure substantially.

Does the calculator handle non-English text?

Character and word counts work for any language, but word counts depend on space-delimited words, so languages without spaces are better measured by character count.
