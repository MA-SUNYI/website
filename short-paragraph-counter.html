Counting the length of short paragraphs is a different task from counting a whole document. Ad copy, captions, product descriptions and slide text all have tight per-block limits, and a counter that reports a document total is the wrong instrument.

Paste the individual block and check it alone. A 280-character caption limit applies to each post, not to a batch of five; a product description field with a 500-character limit applies per product. Counting the whole page and dividing rarely gives the right answer, because blocks are not equal length.

Paragraph counting adds another dimension. Some systems limit the number of paragraphs rather than their length, and trimming by merging two short paragraphs can satisfy both constraints at once. Reading the actual requirement — characters per block or blocks per page — determines which metric to watch.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I count a single short paragraph?

Paste only that block into the counter. Counting the full document and dividing by the number of paragraphs gives an average, not the figure each block must satisfy.

Does the counter treat line breaks as characters?

Usually yes, and some platforms do as well. Removing trailing line breaks is a quick way to reclaim a few characters.

What counts as a paragraph here?

A block of text separated from others by a blank line. Single line breaks are generally treated as continuation rather than a new paragraph.

Why is my caption rejected when the count fits?

Often because the platform counts characters plus a link preview or trailing spaces. Trim a margin rather than pushing exactly to the limit.
