Splitting a bill sounds trivial until tax, tip and uneven orders enter the picture. Splitting the total evenly is fair when everyone ordered similarly; it becomes unfair the moment one person had a $45 steak and another had a side salad.

The most defensible method is proportional: allocate tax and tip in proportion to what each person ordered. If three people spend $40, $30 and $30 on a $100 bill with 20% tax and tip bringing it to $120, the shares are $48, $36 and $36. That preserves the fairness of the underlying orders while distributing the shared costs exactly.

Service charges are the wrinkle worth checking. Some restaurants add a mandatory gratuity to parties above a certain size, and adding a further tip on top is an easy and expensive mistake. Read the receipt before the bill reaches the card reader.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I split a bill with tax and tip fairly?

Allocate tax and tip in proportion to each person's order. If one diner's food was 40% of the total, their share of tax and tip should be 40% as well.

Should we split evenly?

Even splitting is fair when orders are similar. When they diverge significantly, proportional splitting avoids the awkwardness of one person subsidising another.

What if the restaurant already added a service charge?

Check the bill before adding a tip. Automatic gratuity for large parties is common, and tipping on top of it usually duplicates the charge.

How do I handle someone who did not drink?

Exclude alcohol from the shared total and allocate it among those who ordered it, then split the food and shared costs proportionally.
