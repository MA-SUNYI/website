A word count is not one number. Word processors, publishing platforms and search engines each count differently, and the differences are large enough to matter when a limit is enforced.

Most counters split on whitespace, so "state-of-the-art" counts as one word while "state of the art" counts as four. Hyphenated compounds, contractions and numbers with decimal points are the usual sources of disagreement. Twitter/X counts URLs as a fixed 23 characters; academic systems often exclude references; Chinese and Japanese text has no spaces at all and is typically counted by character rather than by word.

For writing with a hard limit, count the way the platform counts. Submitting 1,050 words to a 1,000-word limit because your editor counted differently is a fixable problem only if you notice before the deadline, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a word counter count hyphenated words?

Most counters treating whitespace as the separator count a hyphenated compound as one word. A phrase written with spaces counts as several.

Why do different tools give different counts?

Because they define a word differently. Whitespace splitting, punctuation handling and language-specific rules all vary between tools.

How do character limits work on social platforms?

Most count characters rather than words, and some count a URL as a fixed length regardless of its actual size. Check the platform's own counter before posting.

Does the counter include spaces?

Word counts do not; character counts usually do, and most tools report both with and without spaces because different platforms require different figures.
