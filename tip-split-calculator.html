Tipping and splitting are separate calculations that interact badly when the bill is divided first and the tip applied afterwards. Doing it in the right order — tip on the subtotal, then split — keeps the arithmetic consistent and the result defensible.

On a $120 bill with a 20% tip, the tip is $24 and the total $144. Split four ways that is $36 each. But if tax and a service charge are present, decide deliberately whether to tip on the subtotal or on the post-tax total. Tipping on the post-tax figure adds a few percent, which many people consider fair and others consider excessive — the point is to choose rather than to leave it ambiguous.

Rounding up after splitting is the social friction point. If the split produces $36.20 per person, rounding to $38 covers the gap and usually leaves a slightly better tip. The alternative, collecting exact amounts from four cards, tends to leave a shortfall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Either is defensible. Tipping on the subtotal is the more common convention; tipping on the total adds a small amount and is widely considered generous.

How do I split a bill with tip?

Apply the tip to the subtotal first, then divide the total. Dividing first and tipping after can produce inconsistent amounts for each diner.

What if someone paid with cash?

Collect cash against the shared total before one card covers the rest. That avoids the common problem of the card paying more than its share when cash falls short.

How should I handle an automatic gratuity?

Check the receipt. Large parties often have gratuity added automatically, and tipping again duplicates the charge.
