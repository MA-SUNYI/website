BMR Calculator – Calculate Basal Metabolic Rate Using Mifflin-St Jeor Formula

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) measures the minimum number of calories your body burns at complete rest to sustain vital functions like breathing and cell repair. Many fitness dieters rely on outdated Harris-Benedict equations for metabolism calculations, leading to inaccurate calorie targets for weight loss and muscle gain. This free BMR calculator uses the clinically validated Mifflin-St Jeor formula, the modern gold standard for resting metabolism, to output your exact daily resting calorie burn based on gender, age, weight and height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why is Mifflin-St Jeor better than older Harris-Benedict formulas?

A: Clinical research confirms Mifflin-St Jeor delivers ~10% more accurate resting calorie estimates across all adult age and weight groups.

Q: What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

A: BMR is calories burned at full rest; TDEE adds activity multipliers for exercise, work and daily movement to get total daily expenditure.

Q: How often should I recalculate my BMR?

A> Recalculate after every 3–5 kg weight change to update your resting metabolism baseline for new calorie targets.
