Amazon FBA Fee Calculator

Most Amazon sellers can quote their referral fee from memory and still get their real profit wrong, because the referral fee is the only part of the bill that scales with price. Fulfillment is charged per unit by size tier, storage is charged per cubic foot per month, the fuel surcharge rides on top of fulfillment, and none of those lines care what you sell the item for. This calculator applies every one of them to a single unit, then solves the price at which the product stops losing money.

How the FBA fee stack works

Four charges hit a normal FBA order, and they behave in completely different ways:

Referral fee — a percentage of the total sales price. 15 percent in most categories, 8 percent on electronics and computers, 17 percent on clothing above $20, 20 percent on the first $250 of a jewelry item. It scales with price, so it never squeezes a cheap product harder than an expensive one.

Fulfillment fee — a flat amount per unit set by size tier and shipping weight, from $3.32 for a 2-ounce small standard unit up to $51.32 plus $0.75 per pound for a 71-to-150-pound extra-large one. Since April 17, 2026 a 3.5 percent fuel and logistics surcharge is applied on top of whatever that table says, so a $3.54 fee actually bills at $3.66.

Storage fee — cubic feet multiplied by the monthly rate, multiplied by how long the unit sits there. Standard size is $0.78 per cubic foot from January to September and $2.40 from October to December; bulky tiers pay $0.56 and $1.40. An aged-inventory surcharge starts at 181 days and reaches $6.90 per cubic foot after a year.

Your own add-ons — inbound placement fees, labeling, polybagging, removal or disposal when stock dies. These are per unit and easy to forget.

The formula this page runs is: Net profit = price − referral − (fulfillment × 1.035) − storage − prep − cost of goods.

Worked example: a $29.99 kitchen item, 8 ounces, packed at 8 × 6 × 2 inches, one month of storage, $6 cost, 30 cents of prep. Referral is 15 percent, or $4.50. Small standard at 6 to 8 ounces is $3.54, so with the surcharge it bills $3.66. The carton is 96 cubic inches, which is 0.056 cubic feet, so storage is $0.04. Total Amazon fees come to $8.50, or 28.3 percent of the sale, and net profit is $15.48 — a 51.6 percent margin and a 258 percent return on the $6 you put into the product. Push that same item to 3 pounds and fulfillment alone jumps to $6.90, dragging profit to $12.24 and margin to 40.8 percent.

Read the first column before the second. The small standard tier spans 2 ounces to 16 ounces for 64 cents, which means weight barely matters inside a tier — the tier itself is the price. The expensive decisions are the ones that move a product across a boundary: a box half an inch too tall, a unit that crosses 3 pounds, a set that ships as one bulky parcel instead of two standard ones. Above 3 pounds the large standard fee becomes $6.97 plus 8 cents for every additional 4 ounces, so an 8-pound unit costs $8.57 before the surcharge, about 32 cents for every pound above the first three.

Two pricing quirks are worth knowing. Items under $10 qualify for Low-Price FBA, roughly 86 cents cheaper per unit, which is why a $9.99 product can survive at all. Items over $50 move to a higher rate card — about 8 percent above the numbers above for the same size and weight — so a $55 product pays more to ship than a $45 one in an identical box.

The tiered categories are where sellers misprice most often. A $400 jewelry item does not cost 20 percent in referral fees — it costs $50 plus 5 percent of the $150 above the threshold, or $57.50. Furniture behaves the same way above $200. The minimum referral fee of $0.30 only matters for very cheap add-on items, but it does bite on $2 replacements and samples.

The mistake that costs the most

Pricing from cost of goods with a flat markup. A seller who pays $6 and applies a 3x multiplier lands on $18, which sounds healthy until the fee stack takes $7.46 of it and leaves $4.54 — a 25 percent margin that evaporates the moment a return, a removal order or a month of aged-inventory surcharge appears. The markup method assumes fees are proportional to price. They are not.

The second costliest mistake is treating FBA as free storage. A slow-moving SKU that sits for eight months pays the base rate plus the aged-inventory surcharge, and by month twelve that surcharge is $6.90 per cubic foot per month — on a 0.5 cubic foot item that is $3.45 a month to hold something that is not selling. Liquidation is frequently cheaper than loyalty to a bad product.

Returns deserve their own line in your model. Amazon charges a returns processing fee in categories with free returns, and the unit comes back unsellable often enough that the average cost per sale is meaningfully above the per-unit fee you see here. Sellers in apparel and footwear should treat this calculator's output as a ceiling rather than an expectation.

Where this estimate stops

Amazon changes rates every January and has added mid-year surcharges before, including the 3.5 percent fuel surcharge in April 2026. Advertising, coupons, Vine, removal orders, inbound placement fees for split shipments, and the monthly $39.99 Professional subscription are all outside this model. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the referral fee charged on the shipping the customer pays?

Yes. The referral fee applies to the total sales price, which includes item price, shipping charged to the buyer, and gift wrap. Offering free shipping does not remove shipping from the fee base if you fold it into the price, but it does avoid a second charge on top.

Why did my fulfillment fee rise after a packaging change?

Because dimensional weight and tier boundaries moved. Large standard tiers bill on the greater of unit weight or dimensional weight, and a carton that crosses a tier boundary can add a dollar or more per unit. Shrinking a box by an inch is one of the few cost cuts that requires no negotiation with anyone.

Does the 3.5 percent surcharge apply to the referral fee too?

No. It applies to the FBA fulfillment fee only, and only in the US and Canada. Referral fees, storage fees and closing fees are unaffected, which is why the surcharge shows up as a small line rather than a broad percentage increase.

When does the peak storage rate start and how bad is it?

October 1 through December 31. Standard-size storage goes from $0.78 to $2.40 per cubic foot per month, roughly triple. Two cubic feet held through Q4 costs $14.40 instead of $4.68, so seasonal inventory that arrives in September and sells in November is paying for the privilege.

Can I avoid FBA fees entirely by fulfilling myself?

You avoid fulfillment and storage, but not the referral fee, and you take on shipping, customer service and the loss of the Prime badge. FBM usually wins on heavy or slow-moving items and loses on anything light that depends on conversion rate.

How do I use the break-even price in practice?

Treat it as a floor, not a target. The number covers fees and product cost at zero profit; it excludes ad spend, returns and your own time. A workable listing price is usually 1.4 to 1.8 times break-even for a product that needs PPC to get discovered.
