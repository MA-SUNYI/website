The letter counter reports how many letters a piece of text contains, distinguishing letters from digits, spaces and punctuation. That distinction matters in contexts with strict formatting rules: captions, form fields, business cards and printed materials where a fixed width is available.

Most tools count only alphabetic characters, so "Hello, World! 2026" contains ten letters and ignores the comma, space, exclamation mark and digits. Some contexts require the total without spaces instead, and the two figures diverge by the number of spaces — which in a paragraph of 300 words is around 300 characters. Reading the requirement carefully avoids a rejected submission.

For short writing, a useful habit is to count the longest line rather than the total. Length limits in printed layouts and slide text are usually line-based, and a paragraph that fits overall can still overflow because one line is too long.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the letter counter include spaces?

It reports letters only by default. Counts with and without spaces are usually available separately, since different contexts require different figures.

Are digits counted as characters?

Some tools count digits as characters but not as letters. Confirm which figure your requirement refers to before submitting.

Why does my count differ from my word processor?

Word processors typically report characters including spaces by default. The letter-only count excludes everything that is not alphabetic.

What is the longest line for layout purposes?

Measure the line rather than the total. A paragraph that fits overall can still break a fixed-width layout because a single line is too long.
