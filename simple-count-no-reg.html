Most counting tools want an account before they will count to ten. That requirement makes sense for a service that stores your history; it makes no sense for a counter you will use for five minutes. This version skips registration entirely.

The counter keeps its value while the page is open and forgets it when the page closes. That behaviour is deliberate: nothing is stored, so there is no account, no email, and no record of what you counted. For tallying inventory, attendance or repetitions on a phone, it removes both the friction of signing up and the privacy question of where the data goes.

The main limitation follows from the same design. Anything you need to keep beyond the session has to be written down or entered elsewhere. For longer counting work, that is a small cost; for a quick tally, it is the whole advantage.
