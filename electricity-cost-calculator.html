Electricity cost is the product of power, time and price. A device rated at 1,500 watts running for two hours uses 3 kilowatt-hours, and at $0.16 per kWh that costs $0.48. The arithmetic is trivial; knowing which appliances dominate the bill is what actually changes behaviour.

Ranked by typical monthly consumption: heating and cooling usually lead at 30% to 50% of a household bill, followed by water heating at 12% to 18%, then refrigeration continuously at 5% to 10%. Large single loads matter too — an electric dryer at 3,000 to 5,000 watts used twenty times a month can exceed the cost of every light in the house combined.

Standby power is the invisible category. Devices drawing a few watts continuously can account for 5% to 10% of household consumption. It is rarely worth buying hardware to eliminate it, but it makes the case for switching off or unplugging seldom-used equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the cost of running an appliance?

Watts ÷ 1,000 × hours used × price per kWh. A 1,500 W heater for 2 hours at $0.16 per kWh is 1.5 × 2 × 0.16 = $0.48.

What uses the most electricity at home?

Heating and cooling typically lead, followed by water heating and refrigeration. Together these usually account for over 60% of consumption.

What is a kilowatt-hour?

One kilowatt of power used for one hour. It is the unit electricity is billed in.

Is standby power worth worrying about?

It can be 5% to 10% of usage. Switching off seldom-used devices helps, but heating and cooling remain the larger lever.
