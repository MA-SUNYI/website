Moving Cost Calculator

A moving quote is built from three things: how far you go, how much stuff you have, and when you go. Everything else — stairs, a narrow driveway, a piano — is an adjustment on top. This calculator prices the three main routes side by side (full-service, portable container, rental truck) plus labor-only help, so you can see which one is actually cheaper for your distance instead of assuming.

Result:

Planning estimate, not a quote. Built from published US industry ranges: full-service long-distance at $1.35 per cubic foot plus $0.0022 per cubic foot per mile, containers at $1,450 per unit plus $1.35 per mile, seasonal demand multipliers, and hourly local rates of $150 for two movers and $200 for three.

How the estimate is built

Long-distance moves are priced on volume, not on the number of rooms. Your home size becomes a cubic-foot figure — 300 for a studio, 700 for a two-bedroom, 1,500 for a four-bedroom house — and that volume is multiplied by a per-cubic-foot rate that rises with distance but not proportionally:

Full-service cost = (cubic feet × (1.35 + 0.0022 × miles) + $250) × seasonal multiplier + packing + access + storage

The $250 covers origin and destination handling, which is why short long-distance moves feel overpriced per mile: the fixed part dominates. The rate climbs about 22 cents per cubic foot for every 100 miles, then flattens as the line-haul cost spreads over a longer haul.

Worked example: a two-bedroom apartment, 700 cubic feet, moving 1,100 miles in June. The per-cubic-foot rate is 1.35 + 2.42 = $3.77, so the shipment is $2,639, plus $250 handling, times the 1.08 June multiplier, which comes to $3,120. That is $4.46 per cubic foot and $2.84 per mile. Move the same shipment in January and the multiplier of 0.92 brings it to $2,658 — $462 less for identical work. Add full packing at $1.10 per cubic foot and the bill grows by $770 before a single box is carried.

Under 100 miles the model switches to hourly, because that is how local movers bill. A two-bedroom job works out to about 4.5 hours with a three-person crew plus a $95 truck charge — roughly $995, which sits in the middle of the $700 to $1,400 range most metros quote for the same job.

May through August is the squeeze: school calendars push most families into the same twelve weeks, and neither crews nor trailers can be conjured up for it. DIY is not free

A 26-foot rental truck for 1,100 miles runs about $340 in base rental, $1,155 in mileage at $1.05 a mile and roughly $483 of fuel at 9 miles per gallon, so about $1,980 before you buy a single box. Add $150 of boxes and tape and the bill reaches roughly $2,130 — a third below the $3,170 container price for the same two-bedroom, 1,100-mile move. What that saving buys is two days of driving, a truck you have never parked, and no coverage for your own back or your own furniture.

Labor-only is the middle path most people miss. Hiring a crew to load at the origin and another to unload at the destination comes to roughly $0.55 per cubic foot per end — $770 for a two-bedroom — while you drive the truck. It costs less than full service and saves your furniture from your friends.

What the estimate leaves out

Access is the big one. A 53-foot trailer needs roughly 75 feet of clear approach; if your street, driveway or building dock cannot provide it, the shipment gets shuttled to a smaller truck at $300 to $800 per end. Stairs beyond the first flight, elevators that need reserving, and long carries over 75 feet are all billable line items that a phone estimate usually assumes away.

Specialty items are priced separately and always have been: a piano, a safe over 300 pounds, a pool table, a large aquarium, a motorcycle. So is auto transport, which is a different service with its own carrier. Tips for the crew run 5 to 10 percent of the bill and are not included here. Insurance is its own decision — released-value coverage is free and pays about 60 cents per pound per article, which is why a $2,000 sofa effectively has no coverage unless you buy full-value protection.

Finally, this is a planning model built from published industry ranges, not a carrier's tariff. Two movers with identical inventories can differ by 30 percent based on route density and how full the trailer already is. Use the number to spot an outlier quote, not to win an argument about $200.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do movers quote by cubic feet instead of weight?

Because they estimate from your inventory list, and inventory converts to cubic feet more reliably than to weight. Interstate carriers still bill non-binding estimates on certified scale weight, so a volume-based quote and the final invoice can drift apart if your actual load is heavier than the survey assumed.

How much does a shuttle truck add?

Typically $300 to $800, charged at both ends when a full-size trailer cannot reach the building. It is the most common reason a final bill exceeds the estimate, and the only defense is describing access accurately before booking rather than after the truck arrives.

Do stairs really change the price?

Yes, usually $75 to $150 per flight beyond the first, and more for walk-ups above three floors with heavy furniture. Local crews bill it as time; long-distance carriers bill it as an accessorial. Either way it appears on the invoice, so ask which one applies to you.

Is it cheaper to move on a weekday?

For local hourly moves, yes — weekday mornings are easier to staff and often discounted. For interstate moves the month matters far more than the day, since crew and trailer capacity is the constraint rather than parking.

How many boxes does a two-bedroom apartment need?

Roughly 40 to 60 medium boxes plus 10 to 15 large ones for a two-bedroom with a normal amount of kitchen and closet contents. At $1.50 to $3.00 per box that is $100 to $180, and it is usually the cheapest line in the entire move.

Is a portable container cheaper than full service?

At 500 miles and above it often is, by 10 to 25 percent, because you load it yourself and pay for transport rather than labor. Below 300 miles the delivery and pickup fees dominate and full service usually wins.
