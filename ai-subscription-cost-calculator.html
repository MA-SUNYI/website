AI Subscription Cost Calculator

AI plans are priced individually and billed on different cycles, which is why a stack of five tools feels modest month to month and looks startling in January. Enter each plan with its billing cycle and the hours you actually use it, and this calculator returns one monthly figure, the yearly total, cost per hour of use, and how much of your spend sits in plans you barely open. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

How the Total Is Built

Every plan is first converted to a monthly equivalent, because monthly and annual billing are not comparable as entered. An annual price is divided by twelve; a monthly price is left alone. Tax, if you enter a rate, is applied to each line before the sum.

Monthly cost = price ÷ 12 for annual plans, or price for monthly plans, × (1 + tax rate)

Cost per hour = monthly cost ÷ hours used per month

The yearly total is simply the monthly figure times twelve, which is the number that matters most. A stack of five $20 plans is $1,200 a year, and most people have never added those five lines together.

Worked example. ChatGPT Plus at $20 a month used 30 hours, Claude Pro at $20 a month used 8 hours, and an annual Cursor Pro at $192 a year (a $16 monthly equivalent) used 25 hours. Monthly total $56, yearly $672. Cost per hour comes out at $0.67, $2.50 and $0.64 respectively. The least-used plan is roughly four times more expensive per hour of work than the others, which is the usual finding: the plan that feels harmless is the one subsidising your optimism.

Why AI Spend Creeps Up

Three forces push AI bills upward and none of them announce themselves. The first is trial conversions: a free week becomes a $20 line that nobody remembers adding. The second is overlap: two assistants, a coding tool and a research tool often cover the same three tasks from different angles, and the marginal value of the fourth is close to zero. The third is the upgrade path: hitting a usage cap at 4 p.m. on a deadline makes the $200 tier feel inevitable, and the subscription stays at $200 long after the deadline passes.

The cost-per-hour column is the antidote. It converts a flat fee into a rate you can compare against what you would pay for the same hour of work done another way. A $20 plan used forty hours is fifty cents an hour and easy to defend. The same plan used three hours costs $6.67 an hour, which is more than most people earn, for work that a free tier might have handled. Nothing about the price changed; only the denominator did.

Annual billing deserves its own warning. The discount is real, usually 15 to 20 percent, and the calculation is simple: $240 a year discounted to $192 saves $48. But it is a bet that you will still be using the tool in eleven months, and AI products change shape faster than most software. Three months of monthly billing is cheap insurance before you sign a year.

One habit is worth building: review the stack quarterly, not annually. Cancel anything with no login in the past three weeks, downgrade anything you have used under ten hours in a month, and only then consider whether the saved money belongs in the pro tier of the one tool you actually live in.

What This Calculator Leaves Out

It covers subscription fees only. Usage-based add-ons such as extra credits, API keys used inside a chat app, and connector or seat fees are not included, so a bill with metered components will run higher than the total shown. Team seats are also not modelled: a five-seat plan at $25 per seat is $125 a month, so enter the real amount charged rather than the per-seat price. And the hours field is your own estimate, which is why it is the softest number in the whole calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

I pay for two general AI assistants. Is one of them wasted money?

Only if you use them for the same task. Two general assistants can be redundant if one sits unused for weeks. Cut the expensive per-hour one first, not the cheaper sticker one.

Should I switch to annual billing to save money?

Annual usually saves 15 to 20 percent but locks you in. Switch after a full quarter of steady weekly use, not before.

Does a free tier really replace a paid plan?

Often for a single low-volume task, rarely for daily work. Free tiers cap the newest models and uploads, and the caps cost you time rather than money.

Why does my total differ from my card statement?

Sales tax or VAT is added at checkout in many countries, annual plans are charged once, and app store purchases can carry a markup over web prices.

How should I count hours used?

Honestly. Three sessions a week of real work is about twelve hours a month, not forty. Count only the time you are actively editing, prompting or reviewing output.

Are AI features bundled in software I already pay for worth counting?

Count the bundle once and note what it replaced. Its incremental cost is zero, so it belongs in the comparison as a substitute rather than an addition.
