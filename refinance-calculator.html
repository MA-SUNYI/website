Refinancing replaces an existing loan with a new one, usually at a lower rate or a different term. The decision comes down to one question: does the monthly saving exceed the closing costs, and if so, how long until it does?

A $300,000 balance at 7.5% with 25 years remaining costs about $2,217 a month. Refinancing to 6% over the same 25 years drops that to roughly $1,933 — a saving of about $284 a month. If closing costs are $6,000, the break-even point is 6000 ÷ 284 ≈ 21 months. Plan to stay longer than that and refinancing pays; sell sooner and the closing costs swallow the saving.

Two traps are worth naming. Extending the term — going from 25 years remaining back to 30 — can lower the payment while increasing total interest, which is a payment reduction rather than a saving. And cash-out refinancing converts home equity into debt secured against the house, so a missed payment risks the property.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until refinancing pays for itself?

Divide total closing costs by the monthly saving. A $6,000 cost against a $284 saving breaks even in about 21 months. Staying past that point makes the refinance worthwhile.

Should I refinance if I plan to move soon?

Usually not. If you sell before the break-even point, the closing costs exceed the accumulated saving and the refinance loses money.

Is a lower payment always a better deal?

No. Extending the term lowers the payment while raising total interest. Compare total cost, not just the monthly figure.

What does cash-out refinancing involve?

Borrowing more than the outstanding balance and taking the difference in cash. It converts equity into debt secured by the home, so the risk profile changes substantially.
