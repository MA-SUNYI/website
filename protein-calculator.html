Protein needs scale with body weight and activity, and the widely quoted 0.8 g per kilogram figure is a minimum to prevent deficiency, not an optimal target. For anyone training regularly, needs sit considerably higher.

General ranges used in sports nutrition: sedentary adults 0.8–1.0 g/kg, recreational exercisers 1.2–1.6 g/kg, strength athletes 1.6–2.2 g/kg, and people in a calorie deficit 1.8–2.4 g/kg to preserve muscle while losing fat. A 75 kg person training four times a week falls in the middle band, needing roughly 90–120 g a day.

Distribution matters less than total intake, but it is not irrelevant. Spreading protein across three or four meals of 25–40 g each supports muscle protein synthesis better than a single large serving, which appears to saturate at around 0.4 g/kg per meal. For most people the practical answer is simple: hit the daily total, and do not leave it all to dinner.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein do I need per day?

Roughly 0.8 g/kg as a minimum, 1.2–1.6 g/kg for regular exercisers, and up to 2.2 g/kg for strength athletes. Those in a calorie deficit should aim for the higher end.

Is too much protein harmful?

For healthy kidneys, research does not support harm at these levels. People with existing kidney disease should follow medical advice instead.

Does protein timing matter?

Total daily intake matters most. Spreading it across three or four meals of 25–40 g supports muscle protein synthesis better than concentrating it in one meal.

Can I get enough protein from plants?

Yes, with attention to variety. Combining legumes, grains, soy and seeds across the day supplies the full amino acid profile.
