APY is the annualised return on savings or an investment, and unlike APR it includes the effect of compounding. That difference — typically 0.1 to 0.5 percentage points — is why APY is the number to compare between deposit accounts.

The conversion is direct. A nominal rate of 4.50% compounded monthly produces an APY of (1 + 0.045/12)^12 − 1, which comes out at 4.594%. Compounded daily, the same nominal rate yields 4.602%. Compounded annually, it stays at exactly 4.50%. The nominal rate never moved; only the compounding did.

APY is mandatory disclosure for deposit products in the United States precisely because it makes comparison honest. When a bank advertises 4.5% APY and another advertises 4.5% APR, they are not offering the same thing — the first is what you earn, the second is closer to what you pay.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APY includes compounding and measures what a deposit earns. APR excludes compounding and measures what a loan costs, aside from fees being folded in separately.

Does higher compounding frequency always help?

Yes for depositors, but with diminishing returns. The difference between monthly and daily compounding is usually a few hundredths of a percentage point.

How do I compare two savings accounts?

Compare APY, not nominal rate. APY already reflects compounding, so it is the single number that captures the true annual yield.

Can APY be negative?

APY on a deposit is normally positive, but real APY can be negative when inflation exceeds the nominal yield, eroding purchasing power.
