Volume conversion fails most often not on the arithmetic but on the units. A US gallon and an imperial gallon differ by about 20%, and a US fluid ounce is not the same as an imperial fluid ounce — a recipe followed across the Atlantic without checking will be noticeably wrong.

The core relationships in US customary units: 1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 16 cups = 128 fluid ounces. One cup is 236.6 millilitres, and one US fluid ounce is 29.57 ml. The imperial equivalents are 28.41 ml per fluid ounce and 4.546 litres per gallon, which is where the 20% discrepancy comes from.

For cooking specifically, weight beats volume for dry ingredients. A cup of flour can weigh between 120 and 150 grams depending on how it was scooped, which is a 25% variation — enough to change a cake. A kitchen scale removes the ambiguity entirely, and it is the one upgrade that reliably improves results.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many cups are in a gallon?

Sixteen US cups. One gallon is four quarts, each of two pints, each of two cups.

Is a US fluid ounce the same as an imperial one?

No. A US fluid ounce is 29.57 ml and an imperial one is 28.41 ml, a difference of about 4%. Gallons differ far more, by around 20%.

Why measure flour by weight?

Because volume varies with how the flour is packed. A cup can range from 120 to 150 grams, which changes the outcome of most recipes.

How many millilitres in a cup?

236.6 ml in US customary units and 250 ml in the metric cup used in Australia and parts of Europe.
