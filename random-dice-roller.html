A digital dice roller replaces the physical die when you need a fair result and do not have one to hand — or when the table is not a table. Each roll is independent and uniform, so a six-sided die gives each face a one-in-six chance, exactly as a well-made physical die should.

Multiple dice are where the maths gets interesting. Two six-sided dice do not produce eleven equally likely totals: seven appears six times out of thirty-six combinations, while two and twelve appear once each. The distribution is a triangle that peaks in the middle, which is why games that use 2d6 feel far more predictable than games that use a single d20. Neither is broken; the shapes are just different.

Whether you are rolling for a board game, a tabletop session or a classroom probability exercise, the tool holds no state between rolls. That independence is the property that matters, and testing it is easy: roll a hundred times and check that each face lands near its expected share.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are digital dice rolls fair?

Yes. Each face has equal probability and rolls are independent, so no history influences the next result. The generator is stronger than a physical die, which can be biased by manufacturing or wear.

Why do two dice favour seven?

Because seven has the most combinations (1-6, 2-5, 3-4 and their reverses), six of thirty-six. Two and twelve have only one combination each.

Can I roll a custom number of sides?

Yes, any side count works, including non-standard dice like d8, d10, d20 and beyond.

Does the roller keep a history?

Each roll is independent and nothing carries over. Keep your own tally if you are running a game that depends on cumulative results.
