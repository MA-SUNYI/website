Gift Budget Calculator

Most gift budgets fail in the last week of shopping, when the list is longer than expected and nothing has been set aside for wrapping, postage or sales tax. This calculator starts from the amount you can actually spend, holds back a reserve for the costs around the gift, and turns what is left into a sticker price you can carry into a store without doing arithmetic in the aisle.

How the Gift Budget Calculator Works

Three deductions happen before anything is divided. The reserve comes off the top, money already spent comes off second, and sales tax is handled by working backwards from the all-in allowance to a pre-tax sticker price.

Reserve = budget × reserve percent. This covers wrapping paper, gift bags, cards, tape, postage and the replacement gift for the person you forgot. Standard parcels inside the US commonly run $8 to $15 to ship; a card and wrap add $4 to $8 per gift.

Gift pool = budget − reserve − already spent. This is the money that can actually be spent on merchandise.

Per-person all-in = gift pool ÷ number of recipients.

Sticker price = per-person all-in ÷ (1 + tax rate). At 7 percent tax, every $1.07 at the register is $1.00 of gift, so a $67.50 allowance buys a $63.08 gift.

Worked example: $900 budget, 12 people, 10 percent reserve, 7 percent sales tax, nothing bought yet. The reserve is $90, the gift pool is $810, the all-in allowance is $67.50 per person, and the maximum sticker price is $63.08. Treat that $67.50 as a sticker price instead and the plan overshoots: twelve gifts at $67.50 cost $810 before tax, tax adds $56.70, and the project lands at $956.70 — $56.70 over budget, because tax was charged on top of the pool rather than taken out of it. The $63.08 figure already accounts for that: twelve gifts at $63.08 cost $756.96, tax is $52.99, and the whole project comes to $899.95.

Why the Reserve Is the Part People Skip

The gift is the visible cost; everything around it is the one that arrives later. A $50 present with a $6 gift bag, a $4 card and $12 of postage is a $72 transaction, and none of that appears in the per-person number unless you plan for it.

Tax is not a rounding error. At 7 percent, $800 of gifts carries $56 of tax. At 9.5 percent in parts of California or Tennessee it is $76. The calculator divides by one plus the tax rate rather than subtracting a flat percentage, because that is how a register works.

Shipping scales with the list, not the budget. If half your recipients live out of state, the reserve needs to be closer to 20 percent. Sending one $40 gift across the country can cost a third of its value in postage, which is why a gift card or a shared experience often beats a heavy parcel.

Different relationships deserve different amounts, and that is fine. A useful pattern is to run the calculator two or three times: immediate family at the top allowance, extended family at roughly 60 percent of it, and colleagues or teachers at a fixed $15 to $25. The total is what has to fit, not each individual gift.

Where This Estimate Stops

This is a planning tool, not a ledger. It assumes every recipient gets a gift of similar value and that all gifts are taxed at one rate; in reality some are tax-free clothing in a no-tax state, some are shipped, and some are experiences with no sales tax at all. It also excludes food, decorations and travel, which the National Retail Federation counts inside holiday spending and which frequently exceed the gift line itself. Track actual purchases separately and revisit the reserve after the first week of shopping — if you have already spent a third of the pool on a quarter of the list, the per-person number needs to come down.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I spend per person on holiday gifts?

There is no correct number, only one you can pay without carrying a balance into January. Divide that amount by the list length and hold back 10 to 15 percent for wrapping, shipping and tax. A $900 budget across 12 people works out to about $63 of sticker price per gift.

Should sales tax come out of the gift budget?

Yes, if you buy in a state that charges it. Tax is added at checkout on top of the sticker, so the calculator divides your allowance by one plus the rate to find a price that fits once tax is included.

How much does wrapping and shipping add to a gift budget?

Wrap, bag, card and postage typically add $5 to $12 per gift, and a cross-country parcel often costs $10 or more on its own. The 10 percent default suits local hand-delivered gifts; use 15 to 20 percent if you ship most of them.

What if I have already bought some gifts?

Put the total spent so far in the last field. It is subtracted from the gift pool before the division, so the allowance reflects what is left rather than the original plan.

Is average holiday spending a budget target?

No. Survey averages describe what households intended to spend across gifts, food, decorations and travel, and they vary enormously by income and family size. Treat any published average as context, not as a target to match.

Are gifts tax deductible?

Personal gifts are not. Business gifts to clients or employees are deductible up to $25 per recipient per year under IRS Publication 463, and incidental costs such as wrapping and shipping do not count against that cap.
