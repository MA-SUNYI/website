Wallpaper Calculator

Wallpaper is bought in strips, not square feet, so dividing wall area by roll area almost always leaves you one roll short. This calculator counts the full height drops around the room, adds a trim allowance, rounds each cut up to the pattern repeat, then works out how many strips each roll actually yields. The answer is the number of rolls to order, including a spare for mistakes and future repairs.

How the Roll Count Is Built

The method installers use is the strip method. It ignores open wall area and counts vertical drops instead, because a drop is cut full height whether it lands beside a window or not.

Strips needed = total wall width in inches divided by roll width, rounded up. Total wall width is 2 x (room length + room width) minus any wide openings you choose to skip.

Cut length = wall height x 12 + 4 inches of trim. If the paper has a pattern repeat, the cut length is then rounded up to the next whole repeat. Half-drop papers add another half repeat, because every other strip starts halfway down the pattern.

Strips per roll = roll length in inches divided by cut length, rounded down. A roll only gives whole strips, so a few wasted inches at the end of the bolt cost you a strip.

Rolls = strips needed divided by strips per roll, rounded up, plus the spare rolls you asked for.

Worked example: a 14 by 12 foot room with 8 foot walls has 52 feet of wall, or 624 inches. At a 27 inch roll width that is 24 strips after rounding. Each strip is 96 + 4 = 100 inches. A 27 foot roll is 324 inches long, so it yields 3 strips. 24 strips at 3 per roll is 8 rolls, and 9 with one spare. The same room with an 18 inch repeat gives 108 inch strips, still 3 per roll, so the order does not change. A 24 inch repeat gives 120 inch strips, only 2 per roll, and the order jumps to 12.

Where Most Estimates Go Wrong

The first mistake is subtracting windows and doors. A strip has to run past a window at full height, and the offcut above or below rarely matches the pattern position needed on the next wall. Deduct only a genuine floor to ceiling opening like a patio slider.

The second is ignoring the repeat. A 24 inch repeat on a 9 foot wall turns a 112 inch strip into 120 inches, still 3 strips on a 33 foot roll. Push the same wall to 10 feet and the repeat gives 144 inch strips, so the cell drops to 2. That is why a 12 by 12 room with 9 foot walls takes 10 rolls in a plain paper and 15 in a paper with a 36 inch repeat.

The third is buying across batches. Two rolls from different print runs can differ enough to show at a seam in daylight. Order the full quantity at once, keep the batch numbers, and keep the spare roll in the attic for repairs after a leak or a moved picture hook.

The fourth is paste and prep, which this calculator does not cover. Unpasted paper needs adhesive and a sizing coat, heavy vinyl over rough plaster needs lining paper, and a room with failing paint needs that fixed first. Budget those as separate line items. The estimate here is a paper quantity, not an installation quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many rolls of wallpaper do I need for a 12x12 room?

With 8 foot walls and a 20.5 inch by 33 foot double roll you need 29 strips, each roll gives 3, so 10 rolls and 11 with the spare. Drop to a 27 inch by 27 foot bolt and the strip count falls to 22 at 3 per roll, which is 8 rolls.

Should I subtract windows and doors from the wall measurement?

Usually not. Strips run full height around openings and the offcuts are hard to reuse once the pattern has to line up. Deduct only wide floor to ceiling openings.

Does pattern repeat really change how many rolls I buy?

Yes. Every strip is cut to a whole number of repeats, so a 36 inch repeat on 9 foot walls gives 144 inch strips and only 2 per 33 foot roll instead of 3. That is roughly 50 percent more paper for the same room.

What is the difference between a single roll and a double roll?

US paper is priced per single roll but ships as a double roll, one continuous bolt about twice the length. A 27 inch by 27 foot bolt is a common US double, and 20.5 inch by 33 foot is the metric size many brands sell. Confirm the unit before comparing two prices.

How much trim allowance should I add to each strip?

Four inches, two at the ceiling and two at the baseboard. This tool adds it before rounding up to the repeat, so a ceiling that sags by half an inch does not leave the last strip short.

Can I use this for a single accent wall?

Yes. Enter the wall width as room length, 0 as room width and 0 for openings. The strip count then covers that one wall only. Wallpaper murals sold by the panel are a different product and are ordered by panel count, not rolls.
