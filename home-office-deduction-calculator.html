Home Office Deduction Calculator

There are two ways to write off a home office and they are not close in effort or in result. The simplified method is one multiplication. The regular method uses your actual housing costs, which can be several times larger in an expensive city. This calculator runs both from the same inputs, shows the business-use percentage behind each, and converts the winner into the tax you would actually stop paying.

Result:

Use only costs you actually paid this year. Depreciation on the home is excluded here; the regular method allows it, but it also creates recapture when you sell.

How the Home Office Deduction Calculator Works

Both methods start with the same measurement: what share of the home the workspace occupies. Square footage is the usual way to establish it, and it is the method the IRS expects unless the rooms differ wildly in size.

Business-use percentage = office square feet ÷ total home square feet
Simplified deduction = smaller of (office sq ft, 300) × $5
Regular deduction = business-use % × allowable household costs
Tax saving = deduction × marginal tax rate

Worked example with the defaults. A 150 square foot office in a 1,500 square foot home is 10 percent business use. Household costs of $24,000 rent or mortgage interest, $3,600 property tax, $2,400 utilities, $1,200 insurance and $600 repairs total $31,800. Ten percent of that is $3,180 under the regular method. The simplified method gives 150 × $5 = $750. The regular method is worth $2,430 more in deductions, which at a 24 percent marginal rate is $763 versus $180 in actual tax saved.

Now shrink the numbers. In a low-cost area with $12,000 of housing costs and the same 10 percent share, the regular deduction falls to $1,200, and the gap over the simplified $750 narrows to $450. At $7,500 of costs the two methods are effectively tied. That crossover is why the honest answer to "which method is better" is that it depends entirely on local housing costs and how much space you can legitimately claim.

The simplified method also has a hard ceiling: 300 square feet, or $1,500 no matter how large the office. Someone with a 400 square foot converted garage gets no extra benefit from the extra 100 square feet, which is precisely the situation where running Form 8829 is worth the effort.

Between $8,000 and $15,000 the two are close enough that many filers choose the simpler form and accept a small loss. Above $20,000 the regular method wins by a margin that justifies keeping receipts.

What Counts as an Allowable Cost

  • Rent on the whole home, at the business-use percentage
  • Mortgage interest and property tax, again at that percentage
  • Utilities: electricity, gas, water, sewer, trash
  • Homeowners or renters insurance
  • Repairs to the space itself, and the business share of whole-house repairs
  • Depreciation of the structure under the regular method only, over 39 years

Not allowable: the principal portion of a mortgage payment, lawn care or landscaping that serves the whole property, and any cost already deducted elsewhere. A separate phone line dedicated to the business is deductible in full as a business expense rather than as part of the home office calculation.

Where People Get This Wrong

Assuming the deduction is free money. It reduces taxable profit, not the bill itself. At a 24 percent marginal rate, $3,180 of deduction is $763 of tax, and the other $2,417 is simply cost you already incurred. Anyone who rents a bigger apartment to "get a bigger write-off" is spending a dollar to save 24 cents.

Ignoring depreciation recapture. The regular method lets you depreciate the business share of the house, which feels like a free extra deduction. When you sell, that depreciation is recaptured at up to 25 percent, and it also reduces your basis, increasing the taxable gain. The simplified method avoids this entirely, which is a real argument for it in a home you plan to sell soon.

Claiming a space that is not exclusive. A dining table that becomes a desk at 9 a.m. does not qualify, and a room used for storage of personal items does not either. The exception for a separate structure used in connection with the business is narrow and does not rescue a shared room in the house.

Forgetting the profit limit. The deduction cannot push a business into a loss. In a year with $2,000 of net profit before the home office line, a $3,180 regular-method deduction is capped, and the remainder carries forward under the regular method only. New businesses with thin margins often discover this the hard way.

Two limitations worth stating directly. This tool does not model depreciation, mortgage principal or the interaction with the standard deduction, and it cannot tell you whether your space meets the exclusive-use test. It prices the arithmetic of a claim you have already decided is legitimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the simplified home office deduction per square foot?

Five dollars per square foot, capped at 300 square feet, so the maximum is $1,500 per year. The rate has not changed since the method was introduced, which is part of why it increasingly loses to the regular method in high-cost housing markets.

Do I have to use the same method every year?

No. You can switch between the simplified and regular methods from year to year, though once you use the regular method and claim depreciation, later simplified years do not undo the depreciation you already took. Keep the prior returns so the recapture calculation stays correct.

Can I deduct a home office if I also have an outside office?

Yes, if the home space is used regularly and exclusively for administrative or management work and you have no other fixed location for those tasks. The deduction is then based on that administrative use, not on where you meet clients.

Does a garage or shed count toward the square footage?

A detached structure used exclusively in connection with the business can qualify even if it is not your principal place of business. An attached garage used for storage of business inventory generally does not, because storage must be in a separately identifiable space used regularly and exclusively.

What records do I need to keep?

Square footage of the office and of the whole home, plus receipts or statements for every cost claimed. For the regular method, keep a depreciation schedule from the year you started claiming the space, because it determines the recapture owed on sale.

Is the home office deduction still available in 2026?

Yes for self-employed filers. What remains unavailable is the unreimbursed employee expense deduction for W-2 workers, which has been suspended since 2018 and was not restored. If your employer reimburses you under an accountable plan, the employer takes the deduction instead.
